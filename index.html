    What this data can't see

    Aircraft that don't broadcast. The FAA requires ADS-B Out in most controlled airspace: Class A, B and C, and Class E at and above 10,000 feet. Outside that airspace, some aircraft fly without it, and they never appear here.

    Low or hidden aircraft. This feed comes from volunteer receivers. A receiver needs a line of sight to the aircraft, so low flights behind hills or buildings, and landings at small airports, can drop out.

    Where a plane is right now. Every position is a few seconds to about a minute old. In one test the oldest was 45 seconds. This page shows each plane where it was last heard and says how old that is. The dashed circle on the map shows how far it could have flown since.

    Where it's going. Planes don't broadcast their route. Flight apps add routes from airline schedules. This page doesn't guess.

    Who some aircraft are. Some owners use a temporary privacy address from the FAA's PIA program, so the feed can't match them to a registration or aircraft type.

    Exactly where to look. The angles assume a flat, open horizon and ignore trees, hills and buildings. Altitude comes from the aircraft's own instruments, so angles are close, not exact.

    Exact sound delay. The delay uses a fixed speed of sound, 340 meters per second. Temperature and wind change it.
